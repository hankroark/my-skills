# Worked Example (abridged)

A condensed, illustrative PR/FAQ. Use it as a reference for the *shape* and *voice* of a good draft — concrete customer, problem the customer feels, a solution that beats the current alternative, and an internal FAQ that's honest about risk. It is intentionally not exhaustive.

---

## Press Release

**Headline:** RootCellar — fresh-picked local produce delivered to your door before breakfast.

**Sub-headline:** For health-focused families in dense suburbs who want farmers'-market produce but can't make the Saturday market, RootCellar delivers a curated box of local, in-season fruit and vegetables twice a week between 6 and 8 a.m.

**SEATTLE — March 3, 2027 —** RootCellar today launched a twice-weekly early-morning delivery service bringing produce harvested within 48 hours from farms inside 100 miles. Families wake up to a box on the porch before the day starts, no subscription gymnastics, no 7 p.m. delivery windows that clash with dinner and bedtime.

**The problem:** Parents who care about fresh, local food face a real squeeze. Farmers' markets happen once a week at fixed times that collide with kids' activities. Grocery "fresh" produce is often a week old and shipped from far away. Existing delivery services drop boxes in the evening, when the food sits warm on a porch for hours, and let customers pick anything year-round, which quietly means "not local." Today these families compromise: they settle for grocery produce they don't love, or burn a Saturday morning they don't have.

**The solution:** RootCellar delivers a curated, in-season box before 8 a.m. on Tuesdays and Fridays, sourced only from farms within 100 miles and picked within 48 hours. Today customers use farmers' markets (inconvenient), grocery stores (not fresh or local), or general grocery-delivery apps (evening drops, no locality guarantee). RootCellar is *fresher* (picked-to-porch in under two days), *more convenient* (it's there before the day begins), and *genuinely local* in a way the alternatives don't guarantee.

**Customer quote:** "I'd given up on the farmers' market — Saturdays are soccer and swim. Now I open the door Tuesday morning and there's a box of stuff that tastes like it was picked yesterday, because it basically was." — Maya, parent of two, Bellevue.

**Spokesperson quote:** "We built RootCellar because 'fresh' and 'local' had quietly stopped meaning anything in delivery. We wanted to put real, just-picked food on the porch before the family even wakes up." — Founder, RootCellar.

**Get started:** Enter your zip at rootcellar.example to see if you're in the delivery zone and pick your first box.

## External FAQ (excerpt)
**What does it cost?** Boxes start at $34, with small/large and produce-mix options. No membership fee.
**How does the early delivery work?** Refrigerated vans run pre-dawn routes; boxes are insulated to stay cool until you're up.
**What if I'm not home?** The insulated box keeps produce fresh for several hours; you choose a porch, side door, or building drop point.

## Internal FAQ (excerpt)
**What do customers use today and why is it inadequate?** Farmers' markets (fixed weekly times), grocery produce (old, non-local), evening grocery-delivery (warm porch hours, no locality guarantee). None deliver fresh-local-early together.

**What's the TAM and how did we get it?** ~X households in our metro fit the segment (dense-suburb, kids, income band Y, expressed preference for local/organic). If ~Z% would pay ~$70/week, that's roughly $__M/year locally. *(Open question: the Z% conversion is an assumption — needs a willingness-to-pay survey.)*

**Top three reasons this fails:**
1. Pre-dawn refrigerated logistics make per-box delivery cost exceed what customers will pay — unit economics never close.
2. "Twice weekly, before 8 a.m., local-only" is too rigid; real demand wants flexibility we can't profitably offer.
3. Supply: too few farms within 100 miles can commit to consistent volume and a 48-hour pick window year-round.

**What has to be true to succeed?** Route density high enough to amortize the cold-chain cost; a farm network that can guarantee volume and freshness; and a segment willing to pay a meaningful premium for early + local, not just "fresh."
