# FAQ Rubric & Reviewer's Lens

Use this when building the FAQ section and when self-reviewing (or critiquing someone else's) PR/FAQ.

## Full FAQ question bank

### External FAQs (press & customer voice — answer in plain language)
- What is the price?
- How does it work?
- Where can I buy / access it?
- How do I get help or customer support?
- What's the return policy / warranty / cancellation? (if relevant)
- How do I get started / install it?
- What about my privacy and data? (if relevant)

### Internal FAQs (leadership & cross-functional voice — be rigorous and honest)
**Customer & problem**
- What products or solutions does the target customer use today to solve this problem?
- What problem(s) does this product solve for customers?
- How does this product create value — in what ways is it better, cheaper, or faster than the alternatives?

**Market & competition**
- Who/what are the current competitors?
- How large is estimated demand? What is the TAM (total addressable market)?
- How many customers have this need or problem?
- For how many is the problem big enough that they'll pay to solve it — and how much would they pay?
- How many of those customers have the characteristics/constraints needed to actually use the product?

**Build & risk**
- What happens if a customer encounters edge case X? How does the product handle it?
- What are the hard problems (business model, engineering, legal, UX) we must solve to build this?
- What new capabilities will we need to establish?
- Do we have third-party business dependencies? Why would those partners say yes (what's in it for them)?
- What third-party technologies must work for this to function as promised?
- Any regulatory or legal issues?

**Economics**
- What are the per-unit economics? Expected gross profit / contribution per unit?
- How much upfront investment is required (people, tech, inventory, space)?
- How will we manage the risk of that upfront investment?
- Given the investment and unit economics, how long until profitability?

**The two that matter most — don't skimp here**
- What assumptions need to be true for this product to succeed?
- What are the top three reasons this product will NOT succeed?

## Reviewer's lens — the seven evaluation questions

A good reviewer reads a PR/FAQ to *improve* it, not to rubber-stamp it. Score the draft against these:

1. Is the customer clearly and specifically defined?
2. Is the problem clearly defined, and from the customer's point of view?
3. Does the proposed solution actually address that problem?
4. Would we expect customers to change their behavior to adopt it?
5. On which dimension(s) is it meaningfully better / faster / cheaper than substitutes?
6. Is the TAM — and the payback — big enough to be worth it?
7. What constraints or problems (business, resource, technical, legal) must we solve?

If any of 1-3 is weak, fix those before anything else — the rest doesn't matter if the customer, problem, and solution aren't aligned.

## Common mistakes to catch

- **Vague / universal customer.** "It's for everyone." Force a specific segment.
- **Misaligned solution.** The solution doesn't actually solve the stated problem, or addresses only a sliver of the claimed TAM. Very common — check the problem and solution paragraphs against each other directly.
- **Discounting the competition.** Claiming there's no current solution. There's always a current alternative, even if it's "a spreadsheet" or "nothing." Name it and beat it.
- **Skills-forward thinking.** The idea is shaped by what's easy to build today rather than what the customer needs. A tell: nothing hard has to be solved. If there's no hard problem, it's probably not differentiated.
- **Selling instead of truth-seeking.** The draft reads like a pitch with no weaknesses. A PR/FAQ with no honest failure modes is a red flag, not a strong one.
- **Hand-waved numbers.** TAM asserted, not calculated. Show customers × willingness to pay.
- **Vision too big / too far out.** Aim the announcement ~3-18 months out (24 for complex efforts), a concrete next launch — not "world domination in 5 years." Put the larger vision in a single "What's the bigger vision?" FAQ.
- **It's a plan, not a strategy.** A roadmap or to-do list isn't a PR/FAQ. The document captures the vision and the strategy and the bet, not the project schedule.
- **The "ta-da."** Written in isolation and unveiled for applause. Good PR/FAQs are drafted solo but improved through early, frequent feedback.
- **Great product, wrong problem.** A clever solution to a problem customers don't actually care about (or that's already well solved). Getting the problem right is the whole game.
- **Written too late.** A PR/FAQ written to justify a project already underway isn't doing its job. Its purpose is to decide *whether* to build, before committing.
