# PR/FAQ Template

This is the structure for the final document. The **Press Release** comes first and should fit on roughly one page. The **FAQ** follows and can be as long as needed.

A useful mental model: the press release is written *to the customer*, in plain language, as if the product has already launched. The FAQ is written *to your team and leadership*, and is where the honest, sometimes uncomfortable thinking lives.

---

## Press Release

**Headline**
One line. Names the product so the target customer immediately understands what it is. Customer-facing, not a code name.

**Sub-headline**
One sentence directly under the headline. Names the specific customer segment and the core benefit they get. If this reads as "for everyone," it's wrong — narrow it.

**Summary paragraph**
Open with `CITY — Month Day, Year —`. Use a real, meaningful target launch date. Two to four sentences summarizing the product and the single most important benefit. Write it so a journalist could lift it straight into an article.

**Problem paragraph**
The customer's problem, from *their* point of view, in plain language. What do they struggle with today? What do they use now, and why does it fall short? Make the pain concrete and specific. Resist describing your solution here — stay on the problem.

**Solution paragraph(s)**
What the product is and how it *directly and simply* solves the problem named above. Enough detail to be credible; brief enough to stay sharp. Then explicitly address the alternative:
> "Today, customers solve this with X, Y, or Z. Those fall short because [...]. [Product] addresses this by [...]."
The differentiation — better, faster, and/or cheaper — must be clear here.

**Customer quote**
A fictional but realistic quote from a named-archetype customer describing the concrete benefit they get. It should sound like a real human, not marketing copy.

**Spokesperson quote**
A quote from someone at the company explaining, in an inspiring but honest way, why you built this and what you hope it does for customers.

**Call to action / Getting started**
How easy it is to begin, and where to go (a link / next step). One or two sentences.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### External FAQ
Questions the press and customers will ask. Answer in plain, jargon-free language. At minimum cover:
- What is the price / how much does it cost?
- How does it work?
- Where / how do I get it?
- How do I get help or support?
- (Add product-specific ones: returns, warranty, setup, privacy, etc.)

### Internal FAQ
Questions your own leadership and cross-functional partners will ask. This is the analytical core. Use the full list in `faq-rubric.md`. Cover at least:
- What does the customer use today, and what's wrong with it?
- What problem does this solve, and how does it create value (better/faster/cheaper)?
- Who are the competitors?
- How big is the market? What's the TAM, and how did you calculate it (customers × willingness to pay)?
- What are the hard problems (technical, legal, operational, business-model) we must solve?
- What new capabilities, partners, or third-party dependencies do we need?
- What are the unit economics and the required upfront investment? Payback period?
- Any regulatory or legal considerations?
- **What has to be true for this to succeed?**
- **What are the top three reasons this will fail?**

---

## Open Questions / To Validate

A running list of the unknowns, assumptions, and things you couldn't answer yet. Keeping these visible is a strength — it shows the thinking is honest and points to the next iteration's research. Each item: the question, why it matters, and how you'd resolve it.
