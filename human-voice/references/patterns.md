# Catalog of AI Tells and Their Fixes

Read this for any Revise or Review job. Each entry names the pattern, shows what it looks like, and says how to fix it. The fix is always some version of "say the concrete thing plainly."

A single tell means little; people write all of these sometimes. The signal is density: several categories clustered in one piece. When revising, fix every instance anyway, because each one costs the reader attention.

## 1. Content-level tells

These are the problems the surface tells point to. Fix these first.

**Significance inflation.** Ordinary facts get tied to legacy, broader trends, or pivotal moments.
- AI: "The new intake form marks a significant shift in how the team approaches customer onboarding, reflecting a broader commitment to efficiency."
- Fix: "The new intake form cut onboarding from five days to two."
- Watch for: stands as, serves as a testament/reminder, plays a pivotal/crucial/key role, underscores the importance, reflects broader, marks a shift, setting the stage for, indelible mark, deeply rooted, evolving landscape.

**Generic over specific.** A rare, precise fact gets smoothed into a common, positive one. "Inventor of the first train-coupling device" becomes "a revolutionary titan of industry." The subject becomes less specific and more exaggerated at once.
- Fix: find the specific fact and state it. If you don't have it, use a placeholder.

**Promotional tone.** Neutral material reads like an ad or a travel brochure.
- Watch for: boasts, vibrant, rich, profound, nestled, in the heart of, groundbreaking, renowned, diverse array, commitment to, showcasing, exemplifies, world-class, cutting-edge, best-in-class.
- Fix: describe what the thing is and does. Let the reader decide whether it's impressive.

**Superficial analysis.** Commentary on meaning bolted onto facts, usually as an "-ing" clause: "...creating a lively community", "...further enhancing its significance", "...ensuring seamless collaboration."
- Fix: delete the clause. If the analysis matters, make it a real claim with evidence.

**Vague attribution and inflated consensus.** "Experts argue", "industry reports suggest", "observers have noted", "many believe", "researchers and practitioners". Often one source presented as many, or none at all.
- Fix: name the source ("Gartner's 2025 survey found...") or own the claim ("I think...").

**Vague association.** "Is associated with leadership at", "in connection with", "connected to". The real relationship is hidden.
- Fix: "was CEO of", "taught at", "wrote".

**Canned challenges and outlook.** "Despite its strengths, X faces several challenges..." followed by a vague hopeful ending or a "Future Outlook" section.
- Fix: name the actual problem, its cost, and what is being done. Skip the ending that reassures.

**No firsthand evidence.** Advice anyone with a search engine could write. No story, number, mistake, or decision from the author's own experience.
- Fix: in Revise mode, flag it and leave `[ADD A SPECIFIC EXAMPLE FROM YOUR EXPERIENCE]`. Never invent one.

**Saying the same thing twice.** The point is made, then restated with different words for emphasis.
- Fix: keep the stronger version.

## 2. Vocabulary

These words appear far more often in AI output than in human writing. They cluster: where there's one, there are usually others. The list shifts as models change, so judge by density and by whether a plainer word exists.

**High-signal AI words:** delve, tapestry, testament, pivotal, crucial, vital, intricate/intricacies, interplay, meticulous, underscore, highlight (verb), emphasize, showcase, foster, cultivate, garner, bolster, enhance, elevate, empower, enduring, vibrant, valuable, key (as an adjective for everything), landscape (abstract), realm, align with, resonate with, boasts, multifaceted, nuanced, comprehensive, holistic, robust, seamless, dynamic, transformative, synergy, harness, navigate (abstract), unlock, unleash, leverage (verb), streamline, journey (abstract), additionally, moreover, furthermore, notably, essentially, ultimately, overall (as filler), various (as a vague plural), certainly, absolutely (as an opener).

**Business buzzwords:** insights, actionable insights, the key to, success requires, optimize, maximize, driving impact, enable, solutions-oriented, next-gen, game-changer, future-proof, revolutionary, scalable, disruptive, agile, customer-centric, move the needle, low-hanging fruit, quick wins, win-win, thought leader, best practices (unless citing a specific standard), at scale (without a number), paradigm shift, digital transformation, value-add, circle back, loop in, touch base, sync up, deep dive, bandwidth, double-click on, unpack.

**Fix:** don't swap synonyms. Ask what concrete fact the word was gesturing at, then write that fact.
- "We leveraged robust analytics to unlock valuable insights." → "The query logs showed 40% of searches returned nothing."

## 3. Phrases, openers, and closers

**Filler openers:** "In today's [fast-paced / ever-evolving / digital] world", "When it comes to", "In the realm of", "At the end of the day", "It's no secret that", "The truth is", "Let's face it".
- Fix: delete and start with the second sentence. It's usually the real opening.

**Setup and pivot lines:** "Here's the thing:", "But here's the kicker:", "Here's what most people miss:", "Let me explain.", "Here's why that matters:", "The answer is simple:", "Here's a breakdown:", "Below is...".
- Fix: delete. Start with the substance.

**Fake candor:** "I'll be honest", "I'll be real with you", "Can I be vulnerable for a second?", "And honestly?", "This might be controversial, but", "I'm no expert, but".
- Fix: state the opinion. Real candor doesn't announce itself.

**Hedge stacks:** "It's important to note that", "It's worth mentioning", "It goes without saying", "One might argue", "I was wondering if perhaps it might be possible to".
- Fix: say it, or cut it if it genuinely goes without saying.

**Hype fragments:** "This is big.", "Game changer.", "Let that sink in.", "Read that again.", "Full stop."
- Fix: cut. If the point is big, the specific detail will show it.

**Stock metaphors:** "a testament to", "laying the groundwork", "launching a new chapter", "the energy in the room", "lost the plot", "brutal clarity", "painfully clear", "there's a special kind of magic that happens when", "will never be the same", "ends the era of", "in the ever-evolving landscape".
- Fix: describe the actual thing that happened.

**Canned closers:** "In conclusion", "In summary", "Overall,", "Ultimately,", "Here's to [noun]!", "The future is bright", "Excited for what's next!", "Thoughts?", "Agree?".
- Fix: end on the last useful sentence. If the piece needs a closing beat, make it a concrete next step, an open question you actually care about, or a specific consequence.

## 4. Sentence shapes

**Negative parallelism.** Framing a claim as a correction nobody needed.
- "It's not about the tools. It's about the people."
- "This isn't just a product update, it's a new way of working."
- "We didn't just build a dashboard. We built clarity."
- "...prioritizing speed rather than polish." (the reversed form, common in newer models)
- Fix: state the positive claim. "The tools matter less than who's using them" is acceptable only if someone actually argued the tools mattered most.

**Rule of three.** Reflexive triples of adjectives, nouns, or clauses: "clear, concise, and compelling", "faster, smarter, and more reliable", "creativity, passion, and drive".
- Fix: keep the one that's true and specific, or list the real number of items. Two is fine. Four is fine.

**Stacked fragments.** "More speed. More control. Less waste." / "Every. Single. Day."
- Fix: one real sentence.

**The "-ing" tail.** "...highlighting the need for better tools." "...ensuring everyone stays aligned." "...contributing to a culture of trust."
- Fix: cut, or make it a separate claim with evidence.

**Copula avoidance.** "serves as", "stands as", "functions as", "represents", "marks", "features", "offers", "boasts", "refers to" used where "is" or "has" would do.
- Fix: "is", "are", "has".

**Elegant variation.** Refusing to repeat a noun, so "the plant" becomes "the facility", then "the site", then "the location".
- Fix: use the same term for the same thing. Repetition is clearer, and in technical writing it's required.

**Self-posed question.** "The result? A 30% drop." / "Why does this matter? Because..." / "The skill that will separate winners from losers? Critical thinking."
- Fix: "The result was a 30% drop."

**Contrast formula.** "Prototyping is easy. Production is a whole different game."
- Fix: one sentence about the actual hard part.

**Definition reframe.** "It's an execution problem dressed up as a strategy problem."
- Fix: state the observation and the evidence for it.

**Same first word.** Back-to-back sentences or bullets that all start with "We", "This", "It", or "The".
- Fix: restructure so the important word leads.

**Reading-level creep.** Several three-syllable words in one sentence, or two or more nested clauses. Aim for roughly a 7th to 9th grade reading level in conversational and social writing; technical writing can go higher when the terms are necessary.

## 5. Structure and formatting

**Template structure.** Hook, three-point list, conclusion that restates the hook. Or: context, problem, solution, benefits, call to action, every time.
- Fix: let the content set the shape. Lead with the most important or most interesting thing.

**Uniform paragraphs.** Every paragraph three or four sentences, roughly the same length.
- Fix: let some be one sentence and some run long, based on what each one carries.

**Generic opening.** A claim anyone could make instead of a specific story, number, observation, or position.

**Summary ending.** Restates the body. Cut it.

**Punchy orphan closer.** A one-line mic drop as the last paragraph. Fold it into the paragraph above or replace it with a real final thought.

**Label-colon lists.** Bullets formatted as "**Speed:** We move fast. **Quality:** We don't cut corners." This is the classic chatbot list. Unless you're documenting a real framework with named parts, write prose.

**Bullets where prose would carry more.** Bullets hide the connections between ideas ("because", "so", "but"). Use them for genuinely parallel, separate items: steps, specs, options.

**Formatting excess.** Bold scattered through paragraphs. Title Case Headings. Headings on a short piece. Emoji as bullets or section markers. Horizontal rules between sections. Markdown symbols (`**`, `##`) pasted into email or chat where they won't render.

**Arrow chains.** "Idea → Prototype → Feedback → Launch". Reads like a slide. Write a sentence.

**Em dashes.** AI uses them more than human writers, often with spaces around them, in places a person would use a comma, colon, or parentheses. Default: none.

**Curly quotes in plain-text contexts.** A sign of text pasted from a chat window. Minor, but easy to fix.

## 6. Chatbot residue

Text that was clearly meant for the person chatting with the AI, left in the output.
- "Certainly! Here's a draft:", "Great question!", "Of course!", "You're absolutely right"
- "I hope this helps!", "Let me know if you'd like any changes", "Would you like me to...", "Feel free to adjust"
- "Here's a more detailed breakdown", "Below is a revised version"
- Unfilled template slots: "[Your Name]", "[Company]", "INSERT DATE"
- Disclaimers about knowledge cutoffs or not having access to information
- Notes about what was "preserved", "retained", or "avoided" in the rewrite

Fix: remove completely. In Revise mode, if the user's placeholders are intentional, keep them.

## 7. Plain-English swaps

| Instead of | Use |
|---|---|
| utilize, leverage | use |
| facilitate | help, run, lead |
| commence, initiate | start |
| endeavor, attempt | try |
| ascertain | find out |
| in order to | to |
| due to the fact that | because |
| at this point in time | now |
| in the event that | if |
| a majority of | most |
| prior to | before |
| subsequent to | after |
| with regard to | about |
| sufficient | enough |
| demonstrate, showcase | show |
| authored | wrote |
| relocated | moved |
| passed away | died (unless the context needs the softer term) |
| serves as, stands as | is |
| boasts, features, offers | has |
| a wide variety of | many, or name them |
| going forward | from now on, or cut |
| reach out | email, call, ask |
| circle back | come back to, follow up |
| at your earliest convenience | by [day], or when you can |
